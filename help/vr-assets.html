VR Assets

An "asset" is a piece of art the memory palace can use: an object to stand in a room, a texture for a floor or wall, a skin for a door or a sign. REPchess ships hundreds of them, and Menu → VR Assets is where you browse, edit, and add your own.

Types

An asset's type decides where it can be used, so it's worth picking the right one:

  • Prop: Billboard -- a flat image that always turns to face you. The usual choice for an object standing in a room.
  • Prop: Extruded -- a silhouette given thickness, so it reads as a solid object from the side rather than vanishing edge-on.
  • Surface -- a texture that tiles across a floor or wall.
  • Surface: Facade -- one large, non-tiled image stretched across a whole building front on the street.
  • Surface: Sign skin and Surface: Door skin -- artwork for a castle's street sign or an individual door.

Adding one

New Asset takes an image you upload, or one generated for you (below). You give it an id and keywords -- the keywords are what the search box matches, so they're worth filling in on anything you'll want to find again among hundreds.

Uploaded images are down-converted on import to a size appropriate for how the asset is actually used: a tiled floor texture doesn't need the pixels a building facade does. A resolution choice (Low / Normal / High) sets the tier. Aspect ratio is always preserved -- nothing is cropped or squashed.

The crop editor can trim an image and erase its background (brush or bucket/flood-fill, with undo and redo) before you save it -- worth doing for a prop, since a rectangular photo with a white background reads as a floating card in the room rather than as an object.

Generating images

Generate… in the asset editor makes art from a description. You need an API key from one of two services, and generation is billed to your own account with that service.

  • OpenAI: GPT Image 1, or GPT Image 1 mini, which costs a fraction as much.
  • Runware: a large catalogue of other image models, most of them far cheaper per image. The list offers GPT Image 2 (the best quality, made at 848×848 to keep it cheap, since assets are shown far smaller than that anyway), FLUX.1 schnell (the fastest and cheapest), FLUX.1 dev, FLUX.2 dev, Seedream 4.0 and Ideogram 3.0 (good when the image includes text). Other Runware model… takes any model's ID from its page at runware.ai/models.

Pick a model and the key box asks for that service's key. Each service's key is remembered separately, only in this browser, and sent only to that service. Your last model is remembered too. The GPT models also have a Quality setting (low, medium or high; high to start with), which is remembered as well. When a Runware model reports its cost, the dialog shows what the image cost.

Transparent background is what you want for objects that stand in a room. The GPT models and FLUX.1 dev produce it directly. For the other Runware models the background is removed as a second step after the image is made, which the checkbox notes. It adds a small cost, and edges are occasionally less clean.

A standing instructions field is appended to every prompt. Put your style there ("flat cartoon style, thick outlines, bright colors, no text") so a set of assets generated over many sessions, even with different models, still looks like a set.

Using them

Assets are offered wherever the VR walk needs art -- clicking an empty move-object slot in a room, skinning a floor or wall, choosing a door or sign image. The picker only offers types valid for that spot, so you can't accidentally put a floor texture on a door. See Decorating Your Castle.

Exporting

The manager exports your whole asset collection, either as one JSON bundle or as individual image + metadata file pairs. Menu → Export → Assets does the same thing from outside. This is separate from a full backup -- useful for sharing a set, or keeping your own artwork safe independently of your repertoire.
