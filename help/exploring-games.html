Exploring Your Games

Beyond feeding the move tree's frequencies, your imported games are their own source of insight -- both available from any row's ⋮ menu in the move table.

Find Games

Lists your own games that reached that row's position, with date, opponent, rating, result, and the move you played -- a pawn or knight badge marks whether each came from Chess.com or Lichess, and clicking a row opens it on the original site. Choose Any transposition to match the position by any move order, or This line only for an exact match of your prepared sequence. A summary line reports your overall score from these games, plus how often you actually followed your own standard response when one's set.

Compare Games

Toggles a small table right under the row: one row per move you've actually played from that exact position in your own games, sorted by how often you played each. The header row carries your configured standard response boldfaced, with its own eval alongside if you've already analyzed that node; every other move you've actually played gets its own indented row below it. Click any move to pop up a mini board at the resulting position; click the leading icon to dismiss the table again (same as toggling the menu item a second time). If no games reach this position at all, it says so instead of showing nothing.

If a row has no standard response configured yet, a Use as Standard button appears on the header row instead of a boldfaced move -- a quick way to set a new node's reply from what you've actually been doing rather than typing one in cold.

Analyze Others

The lightning-bolt icon on the header row background-analyzes every OTHER move at once (the standard reply rides along too, if its own eval isn't already at least as deep as what you ask for) -- a quick way to see at a glance whether the moves you've drifted into are still objectively sound. It asks for a depth (its own setting, independent of the deeper default used for children), always runs a single quick line per move at whatever thread count your live analysis board is set to, and jumps straight to the front of the analysis queue -- interrupting anything already running there, since this is meant to be a fast "let me see" rather than something left to churn in the background. Evals fill in as each one finishes; a small hourglass marks a move that's queued or currently being analyzed.

Once every other move has its own eval at least as deep as the depth you last asked for, a summary line appears: "Standard vs. other moves: +0.4" -- your standard's own eval compared against the play-count- weighted average of everything else you've actually played, from your side's perspective (a Black line's numbers are flipped accordingly, so positive always means the standard did better). A positive number means you're measurably losing eval whenever you drift away from your prep; a negative one isn't necessarily bad news -- it can just mean your standard is an intentionally speculative choice (a gambit, say) that scores lower objectively than the safer moves you've sometimes played instead.
