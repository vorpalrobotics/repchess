Exploring Your Games

Beyond feeding the move tree's frequencies, your imported games are their own source of insight -- both available from any row's ⋮ menu in the move table.

Find Games

Lists your own games that reached that row's position, with date, opponent, rating, result, and the move you played -- a pawn or knight badge marks whether each came from Chess.com or Lichess, and clicking a row opens it on the original site. Choose Any transposition to match the position by any move order, or This line only for an exact match of your prepared sequence. A summary line reports your overall score from these games, plus how often you actually followed your own standard response when one's set.

Compare Games

Toggles a commentary line right under the row, listing every move you've actually played from that exact position in your own games -- grouped with a play count, sorted by how often you played each. Your current standard response (if one's set) is boldfaced right in the list rather than left out, so you can see at a glance how often you actually followed your own prep versus drifting to something else. Click a move to pop up a mini board at the resulting position; click the leading icon to dismiss the line again (same as toggling the menu item a second time). If no games reach this position at all, it says so instead of showing nothing.

If a row has no standard response configured yet, a Use as Standard button appears on the most-played move -- a quick way to set a new node's reply from what you've actually been doing rather than typing one in cold.

Each move also has a small board icon to run the engine on it right there, showing a live score alongside the move -- handy for checking whether a move you've drifted into (or your existing standard) is still objectively sound.
