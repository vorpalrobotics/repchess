Board Play Testing

Menu → Test → Chessboard opens the Opening Quiz -- the way to check whether your preparation actually holds up on a real board, separate from the VR memory-palace walkthrough.

How it works

The quiz plays the opponent's side and expects you to find your own prepared standard response at each step. A wrong or missing move counts as a miss; the session keeps a running hit/miss score as you go.

Setting up a session

Before starting, choose the number of questions, the coverage scope (a whole opening system, a single castle, or everything), a maximum depth, and whether to restrict questions to rooms you've marked memorized.

When you're stuck

A wrong move snaps the board back and asks again, so there's a "Give up on this move" button under the board. It shows you the move, counts it as a miss, and carries on down the line rather than ending the question -- the moves after the one you were stuck on are often ones you do know, and they're still worth testing. Giving up after already guessing wrong doesn't count twice; it's one failure either way.

It feeds your review schedule

Missing a move shortens the review interval of the room that move belongs to, so the next thing you'd have relied on getting wrong comes round sooner. The "not the move" message names that room, and the summary lists every room the session moved up -- knowing where a lapse lives is the actionable half, since that's the room to go and stand in. A room you haven't put on the schedule is still named, it just isn't moved. It only ever moves rooms down, never up. See Marking Rooms as Memorized.

After a session

The summary shows your score; you can test again with the same set of questions to see if you've improved, or draw a fresh set from the same scope.
