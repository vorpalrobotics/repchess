Building Your Repertoire

A repertoire is organized into opening systems ("lines") -- each one covers a family of positions that share a trigger, such as "1. d4" or "any first move." Create one from the home screen with the + button: give it a name, a color, and the move(s) that trigger it.

The move tree

Opening a system shows a tree of positions built from your imported games. Each row is a position reached by a sequence of moves; expanding a row shows the replies that have actually occurred, with how often each one was played.

Standard responses

The core idea in REPchess: for any position where it's your move, you pick one reply as your prepared, memorized answer -- the "standard response." That choice is what later becomes a room and a door in the VR memory palace, and what a board-play test will expect you to find.

You don't have to prepare everything at once -- build out a system as deep as you like, and leave the rest for later.

Working in the move table

Each row's ⋮ menu holds everything you can do to that position:

  • Set Standard Response chooses your reply; Add Opponent Move adds a reply the opponent hasn't played against you yet, so you can prepare for it anyway.
  • Set Attributes names the room, starts a castle (see Building a Castle), and holds the note and story. Notes… opens the note directly.
  • Set Move Quality marks the opponent's move !, !!, !?, ?!, ? or ??. The mark shows in the table, on the Opening Graph and on the move's image in VR.
  • Hide this Variation hides a line you don't want to prepare, and keeps it out of the memory palace. The eye button above the table shows hidden lines again, in red, when you want to find one.
  • Focus on this Variation hides everything except that line, and the graph follows the same focus. Copy Moves copies the line as text, and Quiz this Variation opens the board quiz on that line.
  • Browse Games and Compare Games are covered in Exploring Your Games, and the analysis items in Background Analysis. Preview Palace builds a castle's preview; Node Statistics counts what lies below the row. With a castle picked in Show:, it also says how many of those moves are in that castle, which helps when balancing castles against each other. Moves that belong to another castle, or come after a redirect to another castle, aren't counted.

Above the table:

  • Show: focuses the table on a castle, one of its wings, or a named room. The choice is remembered for next time.
  • Compact mode folds a run of forced moves into a single row, so a long line with no choices in it doesn't fill the screen. Its arrow expands just that one line when you need its rows.
  • The other buttons expand or collapse every branch, and open the Opening Graph.

Engine evaluations

The board button at the start of a row opens the live analysis board on that position; hover it for a moment to see the position on a small board. Once a position has been analysed, from there or by the Analysis Queue, the row shows the evaluation, and tapping it shows the engine's lines. See Background Analysis for queueing many at once, and Transpositions Between Castles for the ↪ badge that marks a line leading into positions you already know.

New opponent moves waiting for an answer

Importing games often turns up an opponent move you have no standard response to yet. Until you choose one it gets no door in the memory palace, so a stray blunder never reshapes a room you've already laid out. The room still reminds you, though: while you're in it, a faint image of that move sits in the bottom-left corner of the screen. It shows only with hints on or in edit mode, never while you're testing yourself. Tap it for the room's unanswered moves and how often each has been played against you. From there, Hide dismisses a move for good, the same as Hide this Variation in the move table, and Open in move table takes you to its row to analyse it and choose a reply, which adds the new door.
