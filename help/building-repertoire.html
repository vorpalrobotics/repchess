Building Your Repertoire

A repertoire is organized into opening systems ("lines") -- each one covers a family of positions that share a trigger, such as "1. d4" or "any first move." Create one from the home screen with the + button: give it a name, a color, and the move(s) that trigger it.

The move tree

Opening a system shows a tree of positions built from your imported games. Each row is a position reached by a sequence of moves; expanding a row shows the replies that have actually occurred, with how often each one was played.

Standard responses

The core idea in REPchess: for any position where it's your move, you pick one reply as your prepared, memorized answer -- the "standard response." That choice is what later becomes a room and a door in the VR memory palace, and what a board-play test will expect you to find.

You don't have to prepare everything at once -- build out a system as deep as you like, and leave the rest for later.

New opponent moves waiting for an answer

Importing games often turns up an opponent move you have no standard response to yet. Until you choose one it gets no door in the memory palace, so a stray blunder never reshapes a room you've already laid out. The room still reminds you, though: while you're in it, a faint image of that move sits in the bottom-left corner of the screen. It shows only with hints on or in edit mode, never while you're testing yourself. Tap it for the room's unanswered moves and how often each has been played against you. From there, Hide dismisses a move for good, the same as Hide this Variation in the move table, and Open in move table takes you to its row to analyse it and choose a reply, which adds the new door.
