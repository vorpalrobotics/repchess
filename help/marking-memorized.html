Marking Rooms as Memorized

Decorating a room is only half the job -- at some point you actually know it. Marking a room "memorized" records that milestone.

Toggling the flag

The brain icon in the VR toolbar marks the current room memorized. It's stored per room and persists across reloads, and shows up as a 🧠 glyph on that room's node in the Opening Graph, so you can see at a glance how much of a castle you've actually learned versus just built.

Reviewing what you've memorized

Marking a room memorized also puts it on a review schedule, and a message confirms when its first review falls due: the same day, about six hours later (see The same-day review below). Unmarking a room takes it back off the schedule and discards its review history, which is also confirmed, since that part isn't obvious from the icon going dark.

Memory fades, and the cure is to come back at widening intervals. Once a room is memorized the brain icon becomes its review light: green while the schedule is satisfied, amber when a review is due, red once it's well overdue. Walk in, quiz yourself on the room from memory, then say how it went -- press 1 for recalled perfectly, 2 for mostly correct, 3 for a failure. (Clicking the brain offers the same three choices, plus unmarking the room.)

Each grade moves the room along a fixed ladder of intervals -- 1, 3, 7, 21, 60, then 180 days. A perfect recall advances a step and "mostly correct" holds the room where it is. Two "mostly correct" grades in a row step it back, since an interval you keep half-missing is too long. A failure, at any rung, sends the room back for a same-day review, as if you had just memorized it. Due dates get a small random spread so a wing memorized in one sitting doesn't come due all on the same day forever after.

The same-day review

A room you have just memorized isn't on the ladder yet. Its first review comes about six hours later, on the same day. Grade it 1 or 2 and the room joins the ladder, due again tomorrow. Grade it 3 and it gets another same-day review.

The reason is timing. A review the next day comes too late for a lot of freshly memorized rooms: most of them came back only "mostly correct", and at that point "mostly correct" and "failed" schedule the room exactly the same. A retrieval a few hours in fixes the room while it is still fresh.

A same-day review shows its time in hours rather than as a date: "in 5 hours (around 4:15 PM)". Missing one does no harm. The room simply waits, and whenever you do review it, it joins the ladder from there. A missed board-quiz move doesn't shorten a room that is still waiting for its same-day review, because there is nothing shorter to move it to.

Rooms memorized before this step existed stay on the schedule they were already on.

You don't have to walk into a room to find out it needs attention. A door whose room is due carries an orange DUE tag on its sign, and a long-overdue one a red OVERDUE tag -- so a waiting review is visible from the corridor instead of something you find by trying every door. Elevator panels tag their floors the same way, and a castle's own entry room -- which you reach through a building on the street rather than through a door, so it has no door sign of its own -- carries its tag on the stat strip under the move images outside the front door.

A room you haven't memorized at all is marked differently: a dim grey brain 🧠 in the same spot, the same icon the memorize button itself carries. So a sign tells you one of three things -- you've never learned this one, a review is waiting, or nothing at all, which means it's learned and up to date. Those are the only states marked. "Due soon" isn't, because a marker on every door of a wing you've memorized would be wallpaper and the value of a tag is that it means "stop here"; the brain icon inside the room still reports the full picture. Door signs are hints, so turning hints off for a self-test hides the markers along with the room names.

The review list

The checklist icon in the VR toolbar lists every room that is due or overdue, each with its castle and room name. Pick one and you are walked to its door. A room you grade drops off the list the next time you open it, so a review session is simply "work down the list until it's empty", without leaving the walk.

Two orders are offered at the top of the list, and the one you pick is remembered:

  • By priority puts same-day reviews first, then the most overdue rooms.
  • By castle keeps each building's rooms together, so you can finish one castle before walking to the next.

Rooms that are only due soon appear dimmed below a line. They are worth doing while you are already standing in the palace, but they don't count as work, so the list can still be emptied. A room with no Room Name is listed by its move pair.

Reminders

A same-day review is only useful if it happens the same day, so the app tells you when one comes due:

  • The browser tab shows how many are due, for example "(2) REPchess". This is always on.
  • A desktop notification is optional. Turn it on under Menu → Settings. Your browser asks for permission when you tick the box. You get one notification per room when it comes due, never repeated. If the app is already in front of you, you get a message in the app instead.

If you once told the browser to block notifications for this site, the Settings box is greyed out, with a note on how to allow them again from the address bar.

Reminders only work while a REPchess tab is open somewhere. A background tab is fine, but a closed browser can't remind you, because the app has no server to send notifications from. The notification setting belongs to this browser, so it isn't part of a backup.

Seeing the bigger picture

Menu → VR Schedule shows how much review is coming and when. Menu → Accuracy Report shows how well your reviews are actually going. See Review Schedule and Accuracy.

Grading the same room twice in one visit replaces the first grade rather than stacking on it, so a mis-keyed 1 when you meant 2 is simply fixed by pressing 2. Unmarking a room memorized clears its review history too.

Missing a move in the board quiz

The Chessboard test feeds the schedule too. Miss a move and the room that move belongs to has its review interval shortened by one rung -- if the move you missed sits on a door of the Solarium leading into the Study, it's the Solarium that needs another look, because that's the room whose contents you failed to recall. A move inside a long sequence belongs to whichever room holds that sequence.

It only ever moves a room down. A quiz walks one path, so it asks one door per room: missing it proves the room isn't solid, but getting it right says nothing about the other doors it didn't ask about. So a miss reports trouble and a correct answer does nothing -- advancing up the ladder stays something you grade yourself, standing in the room, having recalled all of it.

One step, not a reset, for the same reason: it's evidence about one door. A room is shortened at most once per session however many times you miss in it, and a room you haven't put on the schedule at all is left alone. The session summary lists every room that moved, with its new due date, and offers a single undo for the lot -- a run you'd rather the schedule didn't remember is easy enough to have, and the schedule is the one thing the quiz leaves behind.

When a room changes under you

A room that picks up a new door (the ⚠️ badge) isn't quite the room you learned any more, so it shouldn't keep coasting on reviews that predate the change. Starting a walk steps any such room back one rung of the ladder and pulls its due date in to match, with a toast saying how many moved. Each change costs one step and only one, however many times you walk past afterwards -- but a second, separate new door is a second change and costs another. Grading the room says you've reviewed it as it now stands, which settles the matter for the doors already accounted for.

Using it while testing

Both the mnemonics quiz and board-play testing can be scoped to "only memorized" rooms, so a review session drills what you've already committed to memory instead of mixing in material you're still building out.

It also protects the room's layout

Marking a corridor or two-track room memorized locks its shape in: if a new variation later adds a reply partway through it, that new reply becomes a side-door instead of splitting the room and reshuffling what you've already memorized. A memorized room with a branch inside it instead (not a simple corridor/two-track) shows a ⚠️ warning glyph on the Opening Graph when a new variation lands in it, since its layout can't be frozen the same way -- worth a look before you trust your memory of it.
