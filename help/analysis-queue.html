Background Analysis

Choosing a standard response is easier with an engine evaluation in front of you, but a deep search on a single position takes real time, and a repertoire has hundreds of them. The Analysis Queue is how REPchess does that work without you waiting on it: you line positions up, and the engine grinds through them in the background while you carry on building.

Queueing positions

From a move row's ⋮ menu:

  • Add to Analysis Queue queues that one position.
  • Add Children to Analysis Queue queues every reply out of it at once -- the usual way to fill in a whole branch's evals before deciding which move to prepare.
  • Add Unanswered to Analysis Queue queues the opponent moves you haven't chosen a reply to yet. With At any level below this node ticked (the first time, it is) it follows your chosen replies all the way down; untick it to look only at this row's own replies. Set Stop after move to leave out anything deeper than where your repertoire ends (it is your reply's move number; leave it blank for no limit). Hidden moves are left out, and so is anything past a room redirected to another castle. The title says how many positions your choices pick up, and they're queued shallowest first. It's also on the move-1 heading's menu, to cover a whole opening system.

Each time you're asked for a depth and a number of lines (how many alternatives the engine should keep track of, up to 6). Deeper and wider costs more time per position; there's no single right answer, and you can queue the same position again later at a greater depth if you want a second opinion. The dialog remembers what you chose last time.

Watching it work

Menu → Analysis → Analysis Queue shows what's pending, with the count in its heading. The item being worked on reports its progress live -- current depth against the target, and the best lines found so far with their evaluations. Rows can be dragged to reorder them, or deleted outright. A Threads control sets how much CPU the background search may use.

Results are written straight onto the repertoire tree as each position finishes, so the evals simply appear on the rows as the queue works down the list. A finished item leaves the queue by itself.

Where the results show up

A finished analysis is kept on its position. Besides the row's own evaluation, every small board in the app shows the saved evaluation of the position it's showing -- score, depth and the start of the engine's line -- whether or not you reached that position by the same move order:

  • the board that pops up when you hover a row's board button, or tap a move in an engine line;
  • the Opening Graph's position preview and room-info panel;
  • the Position & Note board opened from a move pair in VR, and the VR toolbar's mini board.

The position just after your reply gets one too: the engine's line from the position before it passes through your reply, so its score is that position's score. Where nothing has been analysed, the board shows nothing extra. Quiz boards never show one, since it would give the answer away.

It keeps its place

The queue is stored, not just held in memory. Close the tab mid-run and whatever hadn't finished is still there when you come back, and starts again on its own as soon as the engine is ready -- there's no "start" button to remember. An item interrupted partway (by you opening the live analysis board, say) goes back to queued rather than being lost, and resumes when the engine is free again.

A full backup carries the unfinished items too, so restoring one -- on this computer or another -- picks the queue up where it was. The position being analyzed when you took the backup comes back as waiting and starts its search from the beginning.

Jumping the line

Analyze Others -- the lightning-bolt icon in the Compare Games table -- also uses the queue, but puts its work at the front and interrupts whatever's running. That's deliberate: it's a quick "let me see" about moves you've actually played, not something to leave churning. See Exploring Your Games.

A note on VR

Walking the memory palace is demanding on its own, so entering VR stops the queue from starting new work and caps the engine's threads while you're in there. It picks up again when you come out.
