Decorating Your Castle

A generated castle's rooms are structurally complete but empty -- the memory-palace technique needs something memorable in each one, which is what decorating adds.

Edit mode

Inside a room, click the pencil icon to enter edit mode (Esc leaves it again). Each room has one "move-object slot" per prepared reply out of that position -- these are the objects you decorate to build your mental associations.

Assigning content to a slot

Click an empty slot to open the asset picker: assign one of your mnemonic images, a different VR asset, or (if you don't have an image yet) a plain typed placeholder label as a stand-in. An object list can also drive a whole wall's worth of slots at once, in order -- see Object Lists. For where the art itself comes from, and how to add your own, see VR Assets.

Once a slot holds something -- an image or just a typed label -- clicking it instead selects it for moving: arrow keys nudge its position, +/- resize it, and the gear icon that appears (or the Enter key) reopens the picker to change the image/label or remove it.

Recoloring a surface

Clicking a floor, wall, ceiling or staircase opens the same picker, with two extra options. Color… replaces the surface with a flat color outright. Adjust… keeps the texture and changes how it looks: a tint, plus brightness and contrast sliders, with a live preview.

The sliders exist because a tint alone can only ever darken. It's applied as a multiply against the texture, so any tint that isn't pure white takes light out -- and the more saturated the color you're after, the darker the result. Brightness puts that light back, and contrast recovers the detail a heavy tint flattens. They work with no tint at all too, which is what you want for a texture that's simply too dark on its own.

Adjustments belong to that one surface, not to the asset -- the same wallpaper can be warm in one room and cold in the next, and the shared asset is never touched. They travel with the surface into a building default or a saved preset. "Remove all" puts the surface back to the asset's own look while leaving it assigned, which is different from removing the surface, which drops it back to the procedural default.

One thing to know about the preview: it's unlit. The room's own lighting will always read a little darker than the swatch, so if a wall still looks dim after you've matched the preview, nudge brightness up a bit further than looked right.

Fitting the room

Room Geometry lets you resize a room to fit what you've placed in it; the crop editor (brush and bucket erase, undo/redo) helps clean up an image before it goes on the wall.

Knowing when you're done

A room is "fully decorated" -- shown with a 🎨 badge, both in the VR toolbar and on the Opening Graph -- once every move-object slot has a real asset and every named door's destination room has a name.
