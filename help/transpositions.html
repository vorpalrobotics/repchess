Transpositions Between Castles

Chess openings transpose: two different move orders reach the same position. In the move tree that's harmless, but in the memory palace it isn't -- each castle is generated from its own branch, so the same position can end up as two separate rooms in two separate buildings, both of which you'd have to decorate and memorize independently.

Finding them

Menu → Find Transpositions scans every built castle and lists the positions reached by more than one of them. Each group shows the rooms involved, with the castle and move sequence that reaches each.

You don't have to go looking. When an edit or an import creates a new cross-castle transposition, a toast appears in the bottom-right corner saying how many were found, with a Show button that opens the report. It stays up until you deal with it or dismiss it, and once you've seen a particular collision it won't nag you about that one again for the rest of the session -- only genuinely new ones raise it again.

Resolving one

In each group, pick the room you want to keep building out -- usually the one in the castle where the line more naturally belongs, or the one you've already decorated -- and press Redirect Selected. Every other room in that group is redirected to it: their door now routes straight into the kept room in VR, so walking either move order arrives in the same place. Any prepared responses the redirected rooms already had are ported over automatically, so nothing is lost.

You can select in several groups first and resolve them all in one press. A group you leave unselected is left alone -- there's no obligation to resolve anything, and two rooms for one position is a legitimate choice if you genuinely think of the two move orders as different.

What resolving does to the rest of the group

Redirecting also carries everything below the transposition point: once two castles meet at a position, they share every position past it too. Those deeper collisions aren't listed separately -- only the topmost one in each family is actionable, and resolving it settles the rest. If you've seen the report claim fewer groups than you expected, that's why.

When a redirect breaks

A redirect points at a specific room. If that room later disappears -- its castle is renamed or removed, or the variation it sat on is deleted -- the redirect has nowhere to go. REPchess notices and quietly restores the affected rooms to being ordinary rooms of their own, reporting "N rooms restored to normal -- target disappeared". That's a repair, not a loss: the position is still there, it's simply a separate room again, and the report will list it as a fresh transposition next time it's scanned if another castle still reaches it.

Seeing them in the graph

In the Opening Graph's normal view, a room reachable more than one way is coloured distinctly, so you can spot the junctions in a castle's structure without opening the report. See The Digraph View.
