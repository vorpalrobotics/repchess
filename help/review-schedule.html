Review Schedule and Accuracy

Two reports sit in the menu beside Run VR. The VR Schedule answers how much review is coming, and when. The Accuracy Report answers how well are my reviews actually going. The VR walk itself answers the third question, what do I review right now, with its review list (see Marking Rooms as Memorized).

VR Schedule

Its main use is pacing new memorization. If the next few days are already heavy, you might skip memorizing a new room today. Every figure is given in both moves and rooms, since they measure different things. Moves are how much of your repertoire is up for review. Rooms are how much walking it takes, since each room is one visit however many moves it teaches.

  • Show: picks the scope: all castles, or a single castle. All castles is the default, since your workload is whatever is due everywhere.
  • Due now, By tomorrow, Next 7 days are running totals. Each includes everything overdue, because that is the pile you actually face when you sit down.
  • The sentence under them says what a room memorized today would land on tomorrow, and names the heaviest single day in the next month. It states facts rather than making a recommendation, because only you know how much you can get through in a sitting.
  • Coming due splits the schedule into time buckets, from overdue out to 90+ days away, plus the rooms you haven't memorized yet. How well learned shows how many rooms sit on each rung of the review ladder. Each section has a ring beside its bars showing each part's share of the whole.
  • The calendar shows one month at a time, each day shaded by its load. The strip of months above it is the navigation: click the busy month to jump there.

The schedule is a snapshot taken when you open it. Close and reopen it to see reviews you have just done.

Accuracy Report

Every review you grade in the VR walk and every quiz answer is recorded. This report shows what those records add up to. It's the evidence for whether the review intervals suit you.

  • Reviews by ladder rung: one row per rung, with the same-day review on its own row first. Nominal is the interval the rung is meant to be. Ran is how long it actually lasted, which is usually longer, since reviews get done late.
  • Reviews by room size: whether bigger rooms fail more often. Rule this out before blaming a rung's interval for a low score.
  • Quizzes: one row per kind of quiz: Chessboard, Mnemonics and Object Lists.

A score counts each A (1, recalled perfectly) as full credit, each B (2, mostly correct) as 0.6 and each C (3, failed) as nothing. In the quizzes, a right answer counts fully, an "unsure" one 0.6, and a miss or a revealed answer nothing. Partial credit for B is what makes the score useful: a rung that slides from mostly A to mostly B is getting too long before anything actually fails.

To read a rung's score: near the top means the interval could probably be longer; well down the scale means it's too long. A score from fewer than five reviews is shown but marked as provisional, since one or two results say very little. A dash means nothing has been recorded there yet.
