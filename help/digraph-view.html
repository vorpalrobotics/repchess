The Digraph View

The Opening Graph is a network diagram of your repertoire: each node is a position (a "room"), each edge is a move between positions, labeled with how often that reply has actually occurred in your imported games.

Reading the graph

Node labels carry small status glyphs so you can scan a whole castle at a glance: 🧠 means the room is marked memorized, 🎨 means it's fully decorated (every prepared reply has a real image, every named door leads somewhere named), and ⚠️ means a new variation has added a door to this room since you memorized it -- worth a look before you trust your memory of its layout. A room with both memorized and decorated fills in dark green instead of the usual blue, so it's obvious even zoomed too far out to read the glyphs.

View modes

The View dropdown recolours the rooms to answer a different question. Each is a lens on the same graph -- switching doesn't redraw anything, so nothing jumps and any nodes you've dragged stay put.

  • Normal -- colours by role: castle entrances, transposition rooms (reachable more than one way), and the dark-green "memorized and decorated" rooms.
  • Completeness -- how much mnemonic work is left. Rooms needing a word are red, ones with a word but no image orange, ones whose words and images are all in place but whose room isn't built out yet teal, and finished rooms green. This is the "what should I work on next" view.
  • Review -- when each memorized room next falls due, on a green → olive → orange → red ramp from up-to-date to overdue. Rooms you haven't memorized yet stay grey, so an unlearned castle reads as absent from the schedule rather than as finished. The legend also counts how many rooms in the current view are memorized, due, and overdue.

Locked rooms

A room with no moves out of it is one you can only reach through a locked door -- there's no way to walk in, so there's nothing to decorate and no way to mark it memorized. All you can give such a room is its name, on the sign above the door.

So neither lens asks you for work you can't do. Completeness leaves a locked room on its mnemonic score alone -- it still turns red or orange if the move leading to it needs a word or an image, because that mnemonic sits on the door you can see, but it's never marked "not decorated yet". In the Review lens a locked room shows the schedule of the room whose door leads into it, which is the room you'd actually go and stand in to recall that move -- so it lights up alongside its owner instead of sitting grey next to it.

The coverage bars follow the same rule: rooms you can't walk into are left out of the totals, so "rooms memorized" and "rooms decorated" can actually reach 100%.

Focusing a castle

On a large repertoire, use the "Show Castle" dropdown to narrow the view to one castle's rooms instead of the whole tree.

Past 500 moves in the current scope the graph declines to draw at all and says so instead. A whole opening system can run to thousands of moves, which takes a long time to lay out and comes out unreadable anyway -- the nodes end up too small to read, and zooming in loses the structure that was the reason to look. It points at the two ways to narrow the scope: the "Show Castle" dropdown, or Focus on this Variation from a row's menu in the move table. There's a "draw it anyway" link if you want it regardless.

Jumping into VR

Click any node to open its room-info panel -- name, exits, and a "Jump to VR" button that teleports you to that room in the 3D walkthrough, without navigating the street by hand.

It puts you outside the room, a couple of metres back from a door into it and facing the door, rather than inside -- so you can read the sign, recall what's in there, and then walk in to check. That's the position a review is done from; landing in the middle of the room puts the answers on the walls around you before you've had a chance to remember them. A room reachable through several doors is approached through one in its own castle where possible. A castle's entry room has no interior door at all, so the jump puts you on the street facing its building -- which is where that room's name sign and move images live anyway.
