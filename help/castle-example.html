Building a Castle: Example

A "castle" is a building in the VR memory palace that maps one branch of your repertoire -- one room per position, one door per prepared reply. Here's the short path from a line in the tree to a walkable building.

1. Mark a castle root

In the move tree, open a position's Attributes and check "starts new castle," then give it a name (e.g. "Dragon Keep"). Leave the street number on Auto unless you want to place it at a specific spot on the street.

2. Generate the castle

Use "Preview Palace" (the dungeon icon) on that root. It previews a room model built from every prepared standard response reachable from that position -- you can inspect the generated rooms in the Castle Preview before committing to anything.

3. Walk in

From the preview, "Walk in VR" drops you at the castle's entry room on the street. Each door corresponds to a prepared reply; walking through one takes you to the position it leads to.

At this point the rooms are built but bare -- see "Decorating Your Castle" for turning them into an actual memory palace.

One castle, several ways in (wings)

Sometimes two quite different move orders reach what is really the same opening -- a Benoni setup against the London, say, where the two routes never share a single position. Instead of two castles, you can give both the same castle. In each route's first position, open Set Attributes, tick A castle starts here and give it the same castle name. Each becomes a wing of that one castle. The name field suggests your existing castles and says, as you type, whether the name joins one as a wing or starts a new castle. It also warns you when a name differs from an existing castle's only in capitals or spacing, since joining needs the exact name.

Don't use Transposition marker shows castle for this. It only appears where two castles already reach the same position, and only changes which castle's name the transposition marker shows there.

  • One building on the street. Its front door opens into the main entrance. Tick Main entrance in Attributes to choose which wing that is; otherwise it's the shortest one. The street number belongs to the main entrance.
  • The other wings are reached the way you'd really get there: through the door in whichever castle leads into them, which takes you straight into the wing. Each wing's first room also has a back door, to where you came from or out onto the street.
  • No way in. A wing with no castle above it would be unreachable, since the street door goes to the main entrance. Attributes warns you in red when that's the case.
  • If two wings ever do reach the same position, they simply share that room.

In the Opening Graph and the move table's Show: menu, the castle's own entry covers the whole castle: the graph draws every wing, and the move table shows every entrance with the moves below it. Each other wing also has an entry of its own, to look at just that one.

A gatehouse for a whole White system

In a White system the 1.d4 (or whatever the first move is) heading row has Set Attributes too, so the very first move can start a castle. That makes a gatehouse for the whole system: its first room is the position after 1.d4, every line that no other castle claims becomes one of its rooms, and every castle further down gets a door in it that takes you straight there (those castles keep their own street doors too). It's a tidy home for the odd lines that will never earn a castle of their own, without a dozen small buildings on the street. A system with more than one first move can give this to only one of them.

The trade-off: every position in the gatehouse is a room, so it counts toward "To decorate", "Ready to memorize" and the review schedule like any other castle's.

An existing castle can't yet be folded into another as a wing without losing its memorized, decorated and review data -- that needs a "merge castles" tool, which is still to come.
