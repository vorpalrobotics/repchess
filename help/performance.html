Performance

Most of REPchess is instant. The two heavy parts are the chess engine and rebuilding castles. Here is what to expect from each, and what to do when something seems stuck.

The engine's first load

There are two versions of the engine. The faster, multi-threaded one is a download of about 7 MB, fetched each time the page loads (the browser often keeps a copy for a while). On a fast connection that takes seconds; on a slow one, like a phone hotspot, it can take a couple of minutes. Until it arrives, nothing can be analysed. If it can't be loaded at all, the app falls back to a slower single-threaded engine.

Once the engine has loaded, it runs entirely on your computer: analysis needs no network connection. With a connection, the Analysis Queue first asks Lichess's cloud for positions it already knows, which is far faster than analysing them yourself (see Background Analysis).

The Analysis Queue shows which engine you have. Its Threads control appears only with the faster one, and when it's missing a note says why. Reloading the page tries the faster engine again.

Keeping it running in a background tab

The Analysis Queue keeps working while you use other tabs or programs, but browsers save power by slowing down or freezing tabs you aren't looking at, and a deep engine search is exactly the kind of work they target. In Chrome:

  • Energy Saver can freeze a background tab that uses a lot of CPU, so the queue stops until you come back. On a laptop it's often on whenever you're unplugged.
  • Memory Saver can unload a tab you haven't looked at for a while. When you return, the page reloads (which needs a network connection to fetch the engine again), and the queue starts its current position over.

To stop Chrome doing either to REPchess: open Settings → Performance, find Always keep these sites active, choose Add, and enter the site's address (for example vorpalrobotics.github.io). The Energy Saver options are on the same page. Other browsers have similar settings.

On a phone, a tab in the background is paused no matter what, so the queue only runs while REPchess is on screen.

When the queue pauses on purpose

The queue also waits on its own, and says so at the top of its window: while the engine is still loading, while live analysis is using the engine, and while the VR walk is open (so walking stays smooth). It carries on by itself as soon as the reason goes away.

Rebuilding castles

After you change the repertoire, the castles are rebuilt the next time something needs them: opening VR, the VR Schedule, Find Transpositions and a few others. When that takes a moment you'll see Building castles…. The first rebuild after loading the page is the slowest; later ones are much quicker. A rebuild started by the app itself -- the background check for new transpositions -- shows a small Checking for transpositions… note in the corner instead.

Settings → Slowdowns lists every time the page stopped responding for more than a second since it was opened, and what was running then, along with how long each castle rebuild took. If something feels slow, that list is the first place to look.
